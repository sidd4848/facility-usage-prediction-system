# Technical Documentation - Facility Usage Prediction

## 1. Problem framing
A **sample** is "resident R has just made booking k (the *origin*); predict booking k+1 (the *target*)".
Four outputs are predicted: facility, usage weekday, usage hour, and **nudge time** = the predicted
weekday and time of day at which the resident will create the booking (the moment to notify them).
First-ever bookings have no history and are not predicted (cold-start residents enter once they have 1+ booking).

## 2. Data (synthetic, one community)
`data/bookings.csv`: 14,397 bookings, 318 residents, 8 facilities, booking timestamps 2024-01-01 to 2025-06-30.
Fields: `booking_id, resident_id, facility_id, facility_name, booking_ts, usage_ts, duration_min`.
Generated by `fup/generate_data.py` (seed 42). Built-in properties:

* **Imbalance / popularity**: Gym 34%, Badminton 14%, Clubhouse 12%, Pool 12%, Tennis 9%, Yoga 8%, Basketball 7%, Hall 4%.
* **Resident preferences**: peaked Dirichlet facility preferences; per (resident, facility) habitual weekdays,
  a weekday hour and a weekend hour; sequence persistence (repeat boost).
* **Time patterns**: weekend-heavy facilities (Hall, Clubhouse, Pool), facility opening hours, seasonality
  (pool in summer, outdoor courts dip in monsoon, hall in Oct-Dec), Yoga opens 2024-09-01, pool closed Mondays and
  during a Feb-2025 maintenance window.
* **Lead times**: 0-14 days; resident style (planner / mixed / last-minute), longer for Hall and Clubhouse;
  each resident has a booking time-of-day habit. Median lead 1.8 days.
* **Sparsity**: bookings per resident median 30, min 1, 52 residents < 10; staggered joins and leavers.
* **Noise**: 6% exploration bookings, 12% off-habit weekdays, ~28% off-habit hours, 12% random booking times.
* **Changing behaviour**: ~30% of residents change preferences, habit hours and/or booking time mid-history.

Hidden generator flags (`data/ground_truth/`) are used **only** for error analysis, never as features.

## 4. Features and leakage controls
Features come from a single time-ordered walk of the bookings (`fup/features.py`). After booking k is folded
into the running state, the state is **frozen as a snapshot** and becomes the feature source for predicting k+1.

Leakage controls:
1. A sample sees only the resident's own bookings 1..k and community bookings made up to the origin's booking time.
2. No feature uses the target's booking timestamp (it would give away the nudge answer); "now" is the origin booking time.
3. Community priors are expanding/decayed counts as of the origin, not global statistics computed over the full table.
4. Chronological split; hyper-parameters chosen on validation; test untouched until final evaluation.
5. `tests/test_leakage.py` proves: truncating future rows leaves features unchanged; changing the target row's facility/usage
   time leaves its features unchanged; origin precedes target.

Feature groups (recency-weighted with half-lives 45 d resident / 30 d community, plus all-history; sparse counts
shrunk toward the community distribution, which also covers cold-start): resident facility shares, last facility,
community facility trend and facility-to-facility transition, resident-by-facility weekday distribution,
weekday/weekend-specific hour distribution, lead-day distribution, booking time-of-day mean/spread, last-booking
values, activity rate, seasonality (day-of-year sin/cos), time between origin booking and its usage.

## 5. Modelling
Cascade of five scikit-learn `HistGradientBoosting` models: facility (8-class) -> weekday (7-class) -> hour (18-class)
-> lead days (8-class) and booking time-of-day (regressor, absolute-error loss). Stages are trained with true upstream
values (teacher forcing). At inference weekday, hour and lead are the argmax of the mixture sum_f P(f)·P(target | f, ...),
so a wrong top facility does not force wrong day/hour. Nudge = (predicted weekday - predicted lead days, predicted
time of day), with same-day bookings capped before the slot start. Hyper-parameters (4-config grid) were chosen by validation log-loss / MAE.

Alternatives considered (validation, stage-wise): rule baselines (global popularity, resident mode, repeat last),
logistic regression, community-mean timing. Full table in the workbook's *Validation model comparison* sheet.
Gradient boosting was kept because it is the only family combining all signal groups for every stage, but on
validation it is **not clearly better than simple baselines**: facility accuracy HGB 53.7% / logistic regression 56.5% /
resident-mode 53.3%; weekday 48.5% / 48.3% / 48.6%; hour 65.4% / 64.8% / 64.8%; booking-time MAE 63.6 vs 77.4 min
(resident mean). The gains are small because the synthetic habits are largely captured by per-resident frequencies.
Other options not pursued: sequence models (too little data per resident), class-weighted loss (would trade accuracy for macro-F1).

## 7. Error analysis
* **Cascade**: when the facility is right, weekday 49.5%, hour 56.6%, avg 2.39/4; when wrong, 22.5%, 14.5%, 0.51/4.
  Facility errors are the main loss; the most common confusions are Pool, Yoga and Badminton predicted as Gym
  (the dominant facility).
* **Weekday and nudge are the weakest**: residents have two habitual weekdays (and 12% random days), so a single
  weekday guess is capped well below 100%; the nudge needs the weekday, the lead (55% correct) and the time
  (81.9% within 60 min) all right.
* **Changing behaviour**: residents who changed behaviour: 47% facility / 1.35 of 4 vs stable residents 60% / 1.66.
* **Sparsity**: residents with 1-4 prior bookings average 1.18 of 4, vs ~1.57-1.61 with 15+.
* **Imbalance**: Gym is best recalled (70%), Pool (39%) and Hall (45%) worst; rare facilities are under-predicted.

## 8. Limitations
* Data is synthetic; absolute numbers reflect the generator's noise (deliberately substantial), not real behaviour.
  The generator was adjusted once (more habitual residents) and the pipeline's inference changed once (facility mixture)
  after a first test run; the first run's overall score was 34.0%. Test data was therefore seen before these two
  design decisions - no hyper-parameters or further changes were tuned on it afterwards.
* Gain over simple baselines is modest (+4 points overall); the facility stage may be improved by logistic regression.
* One prediction per origin booking; no capacity conflicts, cancellations, or no-shows modelled.
* The time-of-day nudge is a point estimate; an interval or a send window would be better in production.
* Feature state is replayed from the history table; production would need an incremental store.
* Validation set is small (1,331 samples), so hyper-parameter choice is noisy.
